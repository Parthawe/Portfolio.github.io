# Project Story Paths And Pending Work

This is the working editorial map for the currently visible portfolio projects. Hidden projects are intentionally excluded unless they were explicitly kept visible. The goal is not to make every project equally loud. The goal is to make every visible project answer the same basic recruiter question:

What was the problem, what method did I use, what changed, and why does this project deserve to be here?

## Portfolio Strategy

The portfolio should read in three layers:

1. Lead work: projects that can carry the portfolio by themselves.
2. Support work: good projects that prove range, craft, and taste, but should not compete with the lead work.
3. Thin visible work: projects worth keeping visible, but only as concise archive-depth evidence unless more artifacts are added.

The current visible set is good if the hierarchy stays clear. The biggest risk is not quality; it is overclaiming. Some projects have strong artifacts but thin outcomes. Those should be framed as explorations, prototypes, installations, or archive pieces rather than flagship case studies.

## Cross-Project Fixes That Matter

1. Every visible project now has a registry-level fast read: problem, role, team, timeline, outcome, and storyline.
2. Support projects that had no proof stats now have conservative stats sourced from the page content.
3. The project pages already use `ProjectHeader`, so the fast-read narrative appears on page headers without rewriting every page.
4. Only three pages currently use the full `ProjectQuickSummary` module: Mentra, Clawed, and Jugalbandi. That is fine for now; adding it everywhere would make small archive projects feel artificially important.

## Recommended Next Implementation Order

Do not polish alphabetically. Polish by risk.

1. Thin access-limited pages: CueTV, Health App, AI Voice. Goal: make each safe public glimpse feel intentional, not empty.
2. Thin public archive pages: IBM, Office of Diversity, DNA, Code for Build, Sculpture. Goal: compress and clarify.
3. Support pages with strong raw material: Ballah Code, The Point CDC, Enigma, Making of Time, Moniac Machine, Butler's Slice. Goal: add diagrams, proof, and better hierarchy.
4. Large support pages: VJ Parivar, UV Light, Revolving Stage, TEDx. Goal: trim and move the best story earlier.
5. Flagship polish: Mentra, Clawed, Jugalbandi, ExecutiveLens, Raahi, Black Hole, Mentra Brand, The Omakase, TransFi, ZentiPay. Goal: consistency, proof, and final recruiter flow.

## What Not To Do

- Do not unhide the remaining hidden tier-d projects for now.
- Do not make every project a full case study.
- Do not add fake metrics.
- Do not let old student projects compete visually with current AI/product work.
- Do not bury the strongest story under process screenshots.
