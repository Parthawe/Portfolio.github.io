**Useful iPhone inspiration**

- Photos-style editing: persistent canvas, contextual inspector and reversible edits for Typeface. [Apple undo and redo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/undo-and-redo)
- Compact grouped toolbars: current action, current value and one reset; separate advanced controls. [Apple toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- Contextual mobile sheets for evidence, instrument/study choice and selected-point controls; clear dismissal and restored focus. [Apple sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)
- Glass confined to navigation/control surfaces, with opaque accessible fallback. Native iOS refraction and haptics are not guaranteed on this web implementation. [Apple materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)

These are proposed adaptations, not claims that Apple’s native UI should be copied wholesale.

**Visitor risks and smaller issues**

First-time visitors cannot predict Black Hole’s combined slider outcome or Jugalbandi’s silent-by-default play. Mobile visitors scroll between cause and effect. Keyboard visitors lose focus during approval and encounter long glyph-point tab sequences. There was no VoiceOver/NVDA speech test.

Three recurring cognitive-load failures are weak action hierarchy, separated input/output and secondary controls shown too early. Alphabet choices are legitimate; the problem is competing input paths. The most satisfying moments are a real glyph changing, a real recording playing and a citation revealing its source. Prepared text and generic completion messages weaken those moments.

Minor fixes: workshop review-index link, one reset location, consistent evidence captions, honest completion labels and reduced-motion handling for Moniac’s height/width transitions.
